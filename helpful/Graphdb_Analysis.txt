While standard retrieval (Vector RAG) looks for words that sound similar, Graph Retrieval looks for specific things and their connections.

Here is the 3-step process of how it works:

Step 1: Entity Identification (The "Spotter")
When you ask a question, the retriever first scans your sentence to find any "Entities" that exist in our 800 nodes.

Query: "Is metformin ER covered on the Silvr plan?"
The Spotter: It uses a technique called Fuzzy Matching. Even though "Silvr" is a typo, it realizes you mean the PlanTier: Silver node and the Drug: metformin ER node.
Step 2: Context Extraction (The "Neighbor Search")
Once it finds the nodes, it doesn't just look at the word; it looks at everything connected to it. It asks the graph: "Give me everything you know about metformin ER."

The Graph replies: "It's a Biguanide, it treats Type 2 Diabetes, and it has a $10 copay on the Silver plan."
Step 3: Structured Context (The "Cheat Sheet")
Finally, it turns that data into a clean, structured "cheat sheet" for the AI model. It formats it like this:

text
### GRAPH ENTITY: metformin ER (Drug)
Properties: brand: Glucophage XR, drug_class: Biguanide
Relationships:
  - [TREATS] -> Type 2 Diabetes (Condition)
  - [IN_TIER] -> Silver (PlanTier) [copay=$10]

Budget Filtering (Most Powerful):
